City unveils new bike lane plan after months of debate

Cyclists to gain six miles of protected track

A long-awaited redesign of the city's central bike network was presented to the public on Monday, after eighteen months of community meetings, three pilot projects and two earlier drafts that were withdrawn. The plan adds six miles of protected bike lanes, mostly along the river and through two underused park roads, and removes about one hundred and forty car parking spaces.

Why the plan changed

Officials said the latest version is more modest than the original, partly because of pushback from small business owners worried about loading zones. The new design keeps most delivery access in place between six a.m. and ten a.m. and adds several short-term loading bays near shopfronts.

Reaction from riders

Cycling groups gave the plan a cautious welcome. "It is far from perfect," said one organiser, "but it finally gives people who do not yet feel safe on the road a real option." Surveys suggest that more than a third of residents would consider cycling for short trips if a continuous network existed.

Funding

The first phase, expected to start in the spring, will be funded by a federal grant covering about seventy percent of the cost. The remainder will come from the city's transport budget, with a small contribution from a local cycling charity.

What happens next

A formal consultation opens on Friday and runs for four weeks. The council is expected to vote on the final plan in March, with construction beginning by the end of May if approved.
